How I work

Some products have users who can't afford to guess. Traders, engineers, clinicians, compliance teams — people making decisions under time pressure, with incomplete information, where an error isn't an annoyance but a consequence. That's the kind of product I design for.

My sweet spot is data-intensive interfaces for expert users — reconciliation platforms, compliance workflows, engineering portals — where the domain is complex, the brief is often incomplete, and getting the interaction model wrong has real consequences. I've spent the last three years designing for aeronautical engineers at Accenture. Before that: fintech, B2B SaaS, and internal tooling at scale.

I start with research — interviews, shadowing, workflow mapping — until I understand the problem well enough to draw. Then I draw rough wireframes quickly and put them in front of users, because in specialist domains people react better than they describe. I don't need to become the domain expert. I need to understand the workflow well enough to know what the interface has to do. Those are different things.

I typically work through a problem by producing multiple options and mapping the trade-offs — then putting the decision in front of the people who know the context best. My job is to make the choices visible, not to be right.
